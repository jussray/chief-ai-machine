# Chief AI Vision

Chief AI is the founder-controlled prompt and decision operations layer for Se’kret Bip, Founder Control Room, L99, Juss Beautiful Hair, Untold Stories, and future projects.

## North star

Turn founder intent into verified, reversible action without handing product truth, private data, approvals, or long-term memory to any single AI provider.

## What Chief AI should become

- a private, authenticated founder workspace;
- a versioned library of prompts, decisions, reviews, and operating modes;
- a routing layer that uses Claude, Codex, ChatGPT, OpenAI, Anthropic, Perplexity, GitHub, and future providers according to their strengths;
- a durable record of evidence, approvals, outcomes, rollback, and learning;
- a coordination surface that respects project boundaries rather than blending every repo into one optimistic soup.

## What the current app is

The current repository is a browser-delivered vanilla JavaScript prototype. Builder and Freestyle transform stored templates. Browser-local custom prompts and stars are not authenticated cross-device state. The client is not yet a secure private control room and must not be marketed as one.

## Product promises

1. **Founder authority remains explicit.** AI may recommend and prepare work. It does not inherit merge, deployment, billing, credential, or external-communication authority.
2. **Repository reality beats memory.** Connected sources, tests, logs, runtime evidence, and approved decisions outrank summaries and model recollection.
3. **Providers remain replaceable.** Prompts, schemas, data, exports, decisions, and recovery paths stay portable.
4. **Private information stays behind trusted boundaries.** Proprietary prompts, secrets, model keys, customer data, teen data, and privileged operations do not belong in public browser bundles.
5. **Every material action has proof and rollback.** A generated answer, commit, PR, merge, deployment, and healthy runtime are separate states.

## Success condition

Chief AI succeeds when the founder can see what is true, what is risky, what changed, why it changed, how it was verified, and what still requires approval without decoding provider-specific theater.
